# What does a genuine right-wing anarchist think of Javier Milei?

Last week Javier Milei was in Paris - roughly at the same time as the Pope who he despises so much.

Many of my fellow anarchists staged protests - calling him a right-wing extremist or even a fascist.

I still remember my uttermost surprise when Milei won the elections in Argentina. This was the very first time a self-styled anarcho-capitalist had come to power. I knew that this was simply impossible - since very early in my life, I had realized that my political ideology catered to a very small part of the population and was doomed to remain a marginal political faction. It is my judicial affair that drives my political activism - for a cause that has much more chances to have a far broader support - namely digital and privacy rights. And by extension - and given my own ideology - to fight for freedom of speech and recognition of all marginal political affiliations.

So, my first question was, how was Milei able to muster the political support necessary to win the elections in Argentina? What was the situation that made the people to vote for him? It was almost surreal.

The very first thing that caught my attention was his coalition - which despite their party names - was in fact a typical far-right coalition.

So was Milei a demagogue? Very wild idea. Who will claim to belong to a very marginal faction in order to gain power? Milei was first and foremost an economist and his writings were very consistent with right-wing liberalism. There were also a number of other clues:
* He was pro-LGBT rights, something very rare at the far right political spectrum
* He was opposed to many traditional Christian values and even dared call the Pope an imbecile
* He tended to place freedom above order and authority - also a highly unusual stance at the far right

There was only one explanation - Milei was a real libertarian who had made a deal with the devil. He had allied himself with the far right in order to implement his economy reforms.

The people who surround him are almost exclusively classical far right figures. They probably need him because they believe that he will bring economic growth and he will be popular. But morals aside, whether this absurd endeavor can actually succeed is another question.

So, to answer the main question, I - and probably most other right-wing libertarians - have a number of huge issues with Javier Milei.

* There is definitely a very marked militaristic aspect of his policies and this is something that no libertarian will ever accept. He has no choice since his real power base are, in fact, right-wing militaristic extremists. He is trying rehabilitate the military junta in Argentina - one of the most brutal dictatorships in South America during which 30 000 persons disappeared and which fell after they declared an offensive war of conquest against the UK.

* Order and security is good for business, however authoritarianism is definitely not. An unholy alliance between big business and the state leads to the suffocation of small business and ultimately - to economic decline. Enforcing strict rules - including on big business - is the best environment for economic growth. There is not much difference between a private monopoly and a state-owned monopoly - both end being very bad at their work. There are growing reports coming from Argentina about attacks on press freedom and human rights activists. The purges seem to have reached even the House of Argentina in Paris. This is definitely not libertarian.

* In classical libertarian thinking, there are two factions when it comes to social security nets. A more pragmatic one that accepts that a mandatory system in which the state taxes the people and then helps those in need is the least bad and most efficient solution. And a more principled one for which this should be the exclusive domain of charity and especially the Church. If you want to cut all social benefits and you think that the Pope is an imbecile, who exactly will be helping those in need? The human species is both the planet's most aggressive predator and also the one which goes to the greatest lengths of all species when it comes to helping a fallen member to rise. Any system that fails to account for this duality of human nature is simply doomed.

On the other side, I definitely support many of economy policies. He is opposed to state debt and the central bank acting as an endless purse - just like me. I think that switching the currency to dollars is probably not the right decision - as dollars are debt based too.

But on a longer term, there are many moral issues with his terrible coalition and I doubt that they will make it work. Wikipedia classifies his political stance as far right. As more than 9 out of 10 anarchists in the world are lefties, the next time a fellow anarchist tells me that there are very few differences between Milei and open fascism, it will be very difficult for me to not agree. What should have been radical differences have become very slight nuances.

Alas, I am afraid that after his tenure, right-wing liberalism, a political ideology that remains rather unknown to the mass public, will be forever associated with right-wing extremism in most people minds.

Today, right-wing liberalism remains somewhat popular in tech. Many leading figures in tech have, in reality, right-wing liberal ideas. They tend to appear far-right, because they are bad at politics and because they tend to forget that in a real far-right society they probably would not been able to be so successful. So, please, people in tech. Let Milei be a bad example to us all.
